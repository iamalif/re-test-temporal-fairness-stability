## 2. The question

**Anchor [F]:** Ding, Hardt, Miller, Schmidt, "Retiring Adult: New Datasets for Fair Machine Learning", NeurIPS 2021 (oral).
- Proceedings: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/32e54441e6382a7fbacbbbaf3c450059-Paper.pdf
- Supplement: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/32e54441e6382a7fbacbbbaf3c450059-Supplemental.pdf
- arXiv v3: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.04884v3

**Claim under test [F]** (Section 4.1, arXiv v3):
- A gradient-boosted tree (GBM) trained on 2014 US-wide ACSIncome and tested on 2014–2018 loses some accuracy over time. Its demographic-parity (DP) violation stays "stable and essentially constant over time".
- "A classifier that satisfies demographic parity on the 2014 data continues to satisfy the constraint on 2015-2018 data." The same holds for the interventions tested (LFR, ExpGrad, post-processing).
- Appendix D.3 repeats this for equality of opportunity (EO).
- Training and testing on different states "leads to unpredictable results".
- **Open question (Section 5):** "Why does there appear to be more temporal than geographic stability?"

**Working title [J]:** *Does fairness stay put? A pre-registered re-test of temporal fairness stability on ACS 2014–2024.*

**Research questions [J]** (to finalise in the pre-registration):
1. Does a model trained in 2014, and its fairness interventions, keep its fairness properties on 2019 and 2021–2024? This covers DP, EO, and whether constraints certified in 2014 still hold.
2. Is fairness more stable than accuracy, measured on a common scale?
3. Why is fairness more stable over time than across states? Measure both on one scale and decompose the change.

## 3. Central design insight from the independent critique (must shape the paper)

- **[J, logically derived; verify]** For a *frozen* classifier, the DP violation depends only on the distribution of the features within each group. The label never enters.
- **[F]** The folktables ACSIncome label is nominal `PINCP > 50000`, with no inflation adjustment. Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/socialfoundations/folktables/main/folktables/acs.py
- **Consequence:** label drift, including inflation, cannot move DP of a frozen model.
- **Implication [J]:** "DP stayed stable" cannot be the headline; reviewers would call it mechanical. The label-dependent quantities (accuracy, EO, whether constraints hold) and the explanation carry the paper.
- **Supporting theory to cite and turn into pre-registered predictions:**
  - **Asiaee & Aryan 2026 (arXiv 2602.05144) [F as summarised; read in full]:** under base-rate shift alone, equalized odds and EO stay invariant, while DP moves linearly with prevalence.
  - **Chen et al. 2022 (arXiv 2206.00129) [F as summarised; venue unverified]:** within-state 2014→2018 shift is mostly label shift; cross-state shift is mostly feature shift. This is a candidate answer to the authors' open question, and it is testable.

## 5. Original experiment details [F, from the paper and supplement; verify]

- **Task and filters:** label is income over $50k. Filters: age over 16, income over 100, hours worked over 0, person weight (PWGTP) at least 1.
- **DP definition:** the positive rate for White minus the rate for Black.
- **Splits:** an 80/20 random split within each state. The model trained on 2014 US-wide data is tested on 2014–2018.
- **Model:** Appendix C specifies sklearn `GradientBoostingClassifier` with exponential loss, 5 trees and depth 5.
- **Interventions:**
  - LFR from AIF360 with the Adult tutorial settings;
  - ExpGrad from fairlearn with defaults;
  - post-processing by the Hardt et al. threshold method, "also in AIF360".
- **Intervals:** Clopper–Pearson for accuracy, Newcombe for DP. Results are unweighted.
- **Reported figures:** Figure 4 is a figure only. Table 2 reports about 81.7% accuracy, 17.7% DP violation, and 0.3% after post-processing.
- **Underspecified:**
  - the number of seeds;
  - how race groups beyond White and Black enter the methods;
  - the DP post-processing implementation (AIF360 has no DP threshold optimiser).
- **Dataset size differs between versions:** 2018 has 1,599,229 rows in NeurIPS Table 1 and 1,664,500 in arXiv v3. The sandbox build gives 1,664,500, and 1,587,856 for 2014.

**Reconstruction finding (sandbox pilot on original-cohort data) [F]:**

| Model | Test year | Accuracy | DP gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Literal Appendix C GBM | 2014 | 0.705 | 0.023 |
| 50 trees | 2014 | 0.816 | 0.138 |
| 50 trees | 2018 | 0.798 | 0.143 |
| sklearn default GBM | 2014 | 0.814 | not recorded |

## 6. Data facts [F; sources in section 13]

- **Files and licence:**
  - US-wide 1-year PUMS person files exist for 2014–2019 and 2021–2024 (536–600 MB each; the 2024 file was posted 4 Dec 2025).
  - There is no standard 2020 1-year file, only experimental weights, which Census advises not to compare with other years. Exclude 2020. Exclude 2025 too: the 1-year release is undated and delayed.
  - data.gov lists CC0 for every year checked (2014–2019 and 2021–2024). Census terms forbid identifying any individual.
- **folktables:**
  - MIT licence; last PyPI release 0.0.12 (8 Feb 2023); last commit `ebace19` (15 May 2024).
  - Supports years up to 2018 only; RELP is hard-coded. It does not use ADJINC. No fork was found that adds either.
  - It replaces missing values with 0 rather than −1 (issue #39).
  - Issue #22 confirms RELP → RELSHIPP; the maintainer's suggested fix is a plain rename, but the codes differ.
- **Breaks to harmonise:**

| Break | Year | Detail | Handling [J] |
|---|---|---|---|
| Occupation (OCCP) | 2018 | 2010-based codes before 2018, 2018 codes from 2018 onward. 100 codes removed, 151 added, 27 relabelled 2017→18. In the Census crosswalk 56 of 484 codes split; 16.4% of 2014 rows have a split code; 3.1% are unlisted. Census: 2013–2017 and 2018–2022 occupation data are "not comparable" | Crosswalk to a common coarse scheme. Quantify the artefact inside the original window, since the original 2018 point already crosses it |
| Relationship (RELP → RELSHIPP) | 2019 | Spouse and partner split by sex; "roomer or boarder" dropped | Harmonisation table |
| Race (RAC1P) | 2020 | Question and coding changed; Census marks 2021 vs 2019 race comparisons "compare with caution" | Use 2019 as the endpoint for race; make sex co-primary |
| ST renamed STATE | 2023 | Rename | Trivial |
| Place-of-birth codes (POBP) collapsed or deleted | 2017, 2022, 2024 | Two codes changed | Collapse |
| Income rounding | All years | Income above $49,999 is rounded to the nearest $1,000. 2.31% of 2014 rows and 2.55% of 2018 rows are exactly 50,000. Positive rate 0.317 (`>`) vs 0.340 (`>=`) in 2014; 0.369 vs 0.394 in 2018 | Keep `>` as the original. Put any inflation-anchored threshold off the $1,000 grid. Report `>` vs `>=` as a sensitivity check |
| ADJINC | All years | Adjusts income within a survey year only; across years use CPI-U-RS factors as Census recommends | Use for the inflation-anchored label only |

- **Survey weights:** the original is unweighted; Census says weights "must be used" for population estimates. Standard errors use 80 replicate weights: SE = sqrt((4/80) Σ(X_r − X)²). In the pilot, weighting moved DP by about 0.01. Ceross reports that weighting reversed race false-positive-rate comparisons in his exploratory analysis.

## 8. Must-have design elements (ranked, from the independent critique) [J]

1. State the DP invariance up front and design around it. Use the label-dependent questions and the explanation as the headline.
2. Fix the reconstruction rule and tolerances in advance, using 2014–2018 data only.
3. Harmonise features (section 6) and quantify recoding artefacts, including at the original 2018 point.
4. Pre-register estimands, margins, forecasts and the decision rule before any 2019+ file is opened. Margins and yardsticks must come from 2014–2018/19-era information only. Candidate estimands:
   - **E1:** DP drift, unconstrained and post-processed models, at 2019 and 2024.
   - **E2:** does the post-processed model still satisfy |DP| ≤ ε?
   - **E3:** fairness versus accuracy stability on a common scale.
   - **E4:** EO under three label definitions (nominal; inflation-anchored; threshold holding the 2014 base rate).

   Yardsticks:
   - Primary: the median noise-corrected cross-state DP change among large states.
   - Also: the spread of year-to-year changes before 2019, and margins of 1, 2 and 3 pp shown as a curve.
   - Also: trend-forecast intervals for 2021–2024.

   Decision rule: a three-way stable / changed / inconclusive outcome, with Holm correction across about 7 confirmatory tests. With about 1.5M rows a year, sampling precision is not the constraint; the margin decides the result, so fix it first.
5. Decompose each change into:
   - recoding artefacts (raw versus harmonised codes);
   - label definition (the three thresholds);
   - composition change (DiNardo–Fortin–Lemieux reweighting, with Shapley values over feature blocks);
   - change in the label given the features (Cai–Namkoong–Yadlowsky style).

   Report replicate-weight intervals.
6. Weighted and unweighted results, with a rule stated in advance for disagreement.
7. Temporal versus geographic shift on one unit (large states) and one shift-magnitude axis.
8. Sex co-primary; 2019 as the race endpoint.
9. Position the paper against Chen 2022, WhyShift, Ceross 2026, Asiaee & Aryan 2026 and Butnaru & Crișan 2026.
10. Release the harmonised loader and a Zenodo deposit.

**What would sink the paper if left unaddressed [J]:**
- DP equivalence as the headline result;
- raw codes used after 2018;
- a margin set after seeing the new data;
- reading 2019→2021 White–Black changes as population change.

## 9. Angles that would make the work more valuable and more publishable [J, not yet vetted; check each for prior work before adding]

### Core (recommended)
1. **"Certificate expiry" framing.** How long does a fairness constraint certified at deployment remain valid? Estimate a time-to-violation or an "audit horizon" per intervention. This turns a re-test into practical guidance for monitoring and recertification, which is what fairness practitioners and regulators (for example under the EU AI Act) need. Check prior work on fairness recertification intervals.
2. **Theory-confirmation framing.** Pre-register the Asiaee & Aryan predictions (EO invariant and DP drifting under base-rate shift) and Chen et al.'s label-versus-feature-shift explanation, then test them on real 2014–2024 data. Confirming or refuting theory on a large real dataset raises the paper above a re-run.
3. **Benchmark-validity contribution.** Document what folktables users need to know when using it as a temporal benchmark: code breaks, the income heap at the threshold, nominal labels, and that the 2018 point already crosses the occupation break. Release a harmonised 2014–2024 loader. This is useful even if the main results are null, and could be a separate DMLR-style dataset note later, but decide now and avoid splitting one study into two papers (rule 8).
4. **An answer to the authors' open question.** Put temporal and geographic shift on one magnitude axis and decompose both. This gives the paper a clear "why", not just "whether".

### Optional (only if time allows; label exploratory unless pre-registered)
5. **Generality across tasks.** ACSEmployment, ACSPublicCoverage (whose nominal `PINCP <= 30000` filter does move DP), ACSMobility, ACSTravelTime. Check overlap with Ceross, who uses ACSEmployment.
6. **Generality across model classes.** GBM, logistic regression, HistGradientBoosting.
7. **Population versus sample fairness.** Weighted versus unweighted as a survey-statistics lesson for fairness benchmarks; this draws on the researcher's statistics background.
8. **A brief comparison of retraining versus frozen models**, only to connect to Ceross; avoid duplicating his study.

### Avoid [J]
- Making "DP stays stable" the headline.
- Adding deep models; they are irrelevant here and compute-heavy.
- Building a Swedish-registry angle; that data is not public.
- Using 2020 or 2025 data.
